Results and Discussion

Overview

We looked at 55 reports from the International monetary fund across 14 countries. Ten of these countries had a crisis. Four did not. We checked these reports for five things: how sustainable the country's finances are, how stable the banking system is, how urgent the situation is, how much things are getting worse and how vulnerable the country is to outside problems. We gave each of these things a score from 0 to 10. Then we saw if these scores could tell us which countries were in crisis and which were not. We also wanted to know if these scores could warn us about a crisis before it happens.

Crisis vs non Crisis years

When we compared reports from years when a country was in crisis to years when it was not we found that four out of the five things we checked were very different. The biggest difference was in how vulnerable the country was to problems. Reports from crisis years gave a score of 7.8 for this while reports from non crisis years gave an average score of 4.7. The other two things that were very different were how much things were getting worse and how urgent the situation was. The only thing that was not very different was the stability of the banking system. This might be because the International Monetary Fund talks about banking problems in a way that's similar for all countries or because they are careful with their words.

The early warning test

We also wanted to know if reports from before a crisis could warn us about it. We looked at reports from one or two years before a crisis. Found that they did have some warning signs. The biggest warning sign was again how vulnerable the country was to problems. Reports from before a crisis gave a score of 7.7 for this while reports from calm years gave an average score of 4.5. How much things were getting worse was also a warning sign. Not as strong. The other things we checked did not have a warning sign. This tells us that the international monetary fund's reports do have some information that can help us predict a crisis, especially when it comes to problems.

Power

We used each of the five things we checked to try to predict which countries would have a crisis the next year. All of them were able to predict it better than chance. How vulnerable the country was to problems was the best predictor, followed by the stability of the banking system and how urgent the situation was. This is interesting because the stability of the banking system was not very different between crisis and non crisis years. It was still able to predict which countries would have a crisis.

Anchor case: Lebanon

Lebanon is an example of how this works. Reports from 2015 already showed some warning signs especially when it came to problems. These warning signs got stronger over time. Were very high by the time the country had a crisis in 2019. This matches what we found in our analysis: that outside problems are a warning sign for a crisis.

Interpretation and limitations

Our results show that the International monetary fund's reports do have some information that can help us predict a crisis, especially when it comes to problems. This is similar, to what other researchers have found. However our analysis has some limitations. We only looked at 14 countries and 55 reports which is not a lot. We also did not try to figure out if the reports actually caused the crisis or just if they were related to it. The scores we used were generated by a computer program might not be perfect. Finally the way we dated the crises was not always consistent. Despite these limitations we think that our results are reasonable and that the warning signs we found are real.
